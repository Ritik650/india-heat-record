India's Heat Record

1.07 billion station-days of NOAA weather records, analysed in ClickHouse. Heat results cover weather stations with complete records, 1982 to 2024. They describe these stations, not all of India.

Heatwave days did rise at these stations, by about a third to a half from the 1980s to the 2010s, in line with IMD's own count. But the public record carries an undocumented reporting artefact that switches on region by region and inflates the count where it applies. Accounting for it changes the answers to several common questions.

A reporting artefact that follows regional lines

A daily maximum identical to the previous day's, to a tenth of a degree, normally covers about 4% of days. At 23 stations it jumps from 14% in November 2010 to 31% a month later, holds for five years, then falls to 8% by March 2016. No station jumps by 5 to 15 points, so it is all or nothing, and minimums at the same stations never move. 22 of the 23 stations in the area of IMD's Chennai centre are in this group, and none of Maharashtra's 7. These repeats sit on local peaks 6 times as often as on troughs, against 1.3 at clean stations, which is what copying a day's peak into the next day would do. No document found so far describes this, so the cause is inferred.

The questions

Are heatwave days becoming more frequent? Yes, by about a third to a half

Each station is compared with itself, with 1982-89 as 100. Removing every repeat over-corrects, because it also drops genuine hot days, so the fairest test is the 10 stations the artefact never touched. 4 of them fell, so this is a likely rise, not a certain one.

Are nights warming faster than days? No

Overall, days warmed +0.28°C a decade and nights +0.21°C, and nights led at 11 of 31 stations. At stations the artefact never touched the two are level. Each line is one station; a line climbing to the right means nights warmed faster there.

Is summer starting earlier? Not clearly

The first 40°C of the year usually falls around 8 April. Across 22 stations it moved -0.9 days a decade (interval -2.5 to +1.2), far less than the swing between years. Negative is earlier. Shading covers the middle half of stations.

How does India compare? It warmed more slowly than most

Rank 33 of 38 countries at +0.24°C a decade, 1982-2021, under one rule for every country, or 28 using clean stations alone. Lines span the middle half of a country's stations.

Is monsoon rain getting more extreme? No clear sign, 1901-1970

1,714 rain gauges with at least 56 of 70 complete seasons. Station averages per June-September season. Across 76 stations in 2006-2024, heavy days also show no clear change: +0.04 a decade, interval -0.07 to +0.15.

Why rain after 1972 cannot be compared with rain before it

Coverage falls from 2,425 stations in 1970 to 84 in 1973, and the same 70 stations appear to lose 38% of their monsoon rain before recovering. A monsoon cannot do that, so this is a change in reporting. No trend is drawn across it.

India's record is two datasets joined in the early 1970s

Indian stations reporting each year. Rain falls off a cliff in the early 1970s, and temperature effectively starts in 1973, not 1900, so heat questions here begin in 1982.

Data quality

Coverage was measured before any finding was reported. Every check is listed here, including those that passed. The plan corrections record where the data disagreed with the original plan, and the issue log has the per-station detail.

What this cannot tell you

  • These are stations, not India. The file names 3,806 Indian stations, but only 28 pass the bar for heatwave trends, none in the hills, and few have complete seasons after 2015.
  • The clean group is small. 10 stations, with a wide interval. They are mostly in the centre and east, so they cannot stand in for the south or north-west.
  • One source. Every Indian maximum through 2024 comes from a NOAA source its readme says to use with caution, and GHCN-Daily does not adjust for station moves.
  • Not IMD's declarations. IMD declares heatwaves by sub-division, and has since added percentile thresholds. This applies its published station rule to every year alike.
  • Published figures differ in kind. IMD's 536 heatwave days in summer 2024 adds up sub-divisions, so it cannot be compared with these per-station counts.
